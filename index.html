The principle behind every decision

The U.S. Department of Education describes responsible AI in schools like an electric bike, not a robot vacuum: the human stays fully aware and fully in control, while their effort is multiplied. Every AI tool in Broward is meant to be inspectable, explainable, and able to be overridden by an educator.

Our approach

The Artificial Intelligence Leadership Framework

Broward's own AILF replaces the generic industry principles previously shown here. It is a district-wide guide for leaders to use AI responsibly, ethically, and strategically, grounded in the Florida Educational Leadership Standards (FELS 1 through 8) and aligned with the district's Strategic Plan.

"Leading ethical, strategic AI integration to advance student success and excellence." The AILF is not a mandate to use AI tools, nor does it replace district policy, professional judgment, or evaluation frameworks. It outlines effective leadership practices for using AI purposefully to support teaching, learning, and organizational effectiveness.

AILF 1AI for Ethical and Responsible LeadershipFELS 1, Professional and Ethical Norms

Leadership focus: Leaders shall ensure that all AI use is ethical, responsible, and aligned with professional norms to promote the academic success and well-being of all students.

Safeguards: protect student and staff data privacy in all AI applications; identify and mitigate potential bias in AI tools and outputs; establish clear guidelines for responsible AI use; promote equitable access to AI resources and opportunities.

AILF 2AI for Vision, Strategy, and Change ManagementFELS 2, Vision and Mission

Leadership focus: Leaders shall establish, communicate, and sustain a shared vision for the strategic and ethical use of AI, aligned with the school and district mission, instructional priorities, and goals for student success.

Safeguards: support development and communication of AI expectations aligned to the School Improvement Plan and district strategic priorities; involve staff, students, and stakeholders; enhance teaching, learning, and student outcomes; monitor progress toward defined AI integration goals.

AILF 3AI for Organizational ManagementFELS 3, School Operations, Management, and Safety

Leadership focus: Leaders shall leverage AI to strengthen organizational systems, increase operational efficiency, and support strategic decision-making aligned to a safe school environment, supervision, emergency procedures, and student and staff well-being.

Safeguards: improve scheduling, communication, and resource allocation; integrate AI insights into school improvement planning and data review; streamline operational tasks to maximize time for instructional leadership; identify inefficiencies and data-informed improvements.

AILF 4AI for Student Learning and Continuous School ImprovementFELS 4

Leadership focus: Leaders shall strategically utilize AI to enhance data-informed decision-making, strengthen intervention systems, and improve measurable student outcomes for all student groups.

Safeguards: systematically analyze student performance, subgroup, and longitudinal data; inform intervention design and progress monitoring; conduct data analysis aligned to School Improvement Plan goals; support equitable access to rigorous learning for all students.

AILF 5AI for Inclusive and Supportive Learning EnvironmentsFELS 5, Learning Environment

Leadership focus: Leaders shall ensure that AI is used to cultivate a caring, inclusive, rigorous, and supportive learning environment that promotes student belonging, engagement, well-being, safety, and academic success for all students.

Safeguards: promote a positive, inclusive, student-centered culture; reinforce high expectations for behavior and engagement; monitor environments for rigor, safety, and respect; support staff in addressing social, emotional, behavioral, and academic needs.

AILF 6AI for Capacity Building and Talent DevelopmentFELS 6, Recruitment and Professional Learning

Leadership focus: Leaders shall ensure that AI is strategically used to strengthen recruitment, professional learning, instructional coaching, and staff support systems that build the capacity of instructional personnel to improve teaching, learning, and student outcomes.

Safeguards: differentiate professional learning based on teacher needs and experience; design job-embedded learning aligned to school goals; strengthen instructional coaching cycles; support recruitment, onboarding, and retention systems.

AILF 7AI for Leadership and InnovationFELS 7, Building Leadership Expertise

Leadership focus: Leaders shall ensure that AI is strategically used to strengthen leadership expertise, distributed leadership structures, succession planning, and organizational leadership capacity to support long-term school improvement and sustainability.

Safeguards: strengthen distributed leadership and clarify responsibilities; support assistant principal, coach, and teacher-leader growth; support succession planning and leadership continuity; analyze leadership challenges through multiple lenses.

AILF 8AI for Communication and PartnershipsFELS 8, Meaningful Parent, Family and Community Engagement

Leadership focus: Leaders shall ensure that AI is used to strengthen reciprocal communication, build trusting relationships, and promote meaningful collaboration with families and the community to support student success and well-being.

Safeguards: communicate clearly, consistently, and respectfully with families; differentiate communication to meet diverse family needs; analyze stakeholder feedback to inform decisions; establish systems for two-way communication and ongoing collaboration.

Human leadership, always

Across all eight domains, the AILF is explicit that AI tools, including Microsoft Copilot, are meant to enhance, not replace, human leadership, professional expertise, ethical judgment, and student-centered decision-making. Final decisions remain human.

The full AILF document, including detailed classroom and leadership examples for each domain (walkthrough tools, PLC agendas, coaching notes, family communication, and more), is available from BCPS as a downloadable reference for administrators.

Approved tools

Only reviewed, district-approved tools

Broward uses an approved-tools-only model. Student and staff information is never entered into tools that have not been reviewed. Each approved tool clears privacy, security, and accessibility review first.

How a tool gets approved

Every tool passes the same review before it reaches a classroom.

1

Privacy review

Signed data privacy agreement, FERPA check, and an independent privacy rating.

2

Security review

Independent security audit, encryption, and breach-notification terms.

3

Accessibility review

Tested against WCAG 2.1 AA, the standard accessibility rulebook, as required under ADA Title II, so students using screen readers or other assistive technology can use it too.

Approval & publish

Frequently asked questions

1. What is Artificial Intelligence?
Artificial Intelligence, often called AI, is technology that can help people organize information, identify patterns, generate ideas, draft content, provide feedback, and support learning activities. Artificial Intelligence is a tool used to support teachers and students. It does not replace the educational relationship among teachers, students, and families. Teachers remain central to learning.
2. Why is the district talking about Artificial Intelligence?
Artificial Intelligence is already part of daily life, the workplace, and future career pathways. Broward County Public Schools wants students to understand these tools and learn to use them safely, responsibly, ethically, and with adult guidance.
3. What does “Human Intelligence First, Artificial Intelligence Second” mean?
It means people remain the decision makers. Teachers lead instruction. Students do the thinking. Families remain partners. Artificial Intelligence can support learning, but it should never replace human judgment, relationships, creativity, care, or responsibility.
4. Will Artificial Intelligence replace teachers?
No. Teachers remain at the center of learning. Teachers make instructional decisions, build relationships, provide feedback, support students, and determine how tools are used in the classroom.
5. Are elementary students using district-approved Artificial Intelligence tools?
No. Broward County Public Schools does not currently use or provide district-approved, student-facing Artificial Intelligence tools to students in kindergarten through grade 5. Because students may still encounter public tools outside district-controlled settings, families should talk with their children about safe and responsible use.
6. Will elementary students learn about Artificial Intelligence?
Elementary students may receive age-appropriate instruction about Artificial Intelligence literacy and digital citizenship, such as how technology can generate information, why information must be checked, and how to use technology safely. The specific curriculum and timing are still being reviewed. This instruction does not mean elementary students are given access to district-approved student-facing Artificial Intelligence tools. Because students may still encounter public tools outside district-controlled settings, families should talk with their children about safe and responsible use.
7. What is the difference between teacher-facing and student-facing tools?
Teacher-facing tools help educators with tasks such as planning, creating resources, communicating, and differentiating instruction. Student-facing tools are tools students may interact with directly, but only in approved settings, with teacher direction and monitoring.
8. Are teachers required to use district Artificial Intelligence tools?
No. Based on current district practice, teachers’ use of district Artificial Intelligence tools is optional. Use must follow district guidance, completion of required training, have instructional purpose, and be guided by the teacher’s professional judgment.
9. How are teachers trained?
Teachers receive training and support through a variety of formats, including online, blended, and in-person sessions, as well as learning communities and coaching. These opportunities address numerous content areas and support teacher growth and development. Specific to Artificial Intelligence, current and planned training includes responsible use, ethics, privacy, instructional best practices, human oversight, and guidance on when Artificial Intelligence should and should not be used.
10. What is Khanmigo?
Khanmigo is an Artificial Intelligence-supported tutoring and guidance tool through Khan Academy. It prompts students to think through problems instead of simply providing answers. It supports student thinking through guiding prompts, while teachers remain responsible for directing its instructional use. Khanmigo acts like a tutor and cannot be used as a shortcut to get answers.
11. What is HMH Writable or Revision Aid?
HMH Writable, including Revision Aid, supports writing and revision. It can provide feedback while students are drafting so they can revise before submitting work to the teacher. It is not a conversational Artificial Intelligence tool, and it does not replace the teacher’s review or feedback.
12. What is MagicSchool?
MagicSchool is an Artificial Intelligence platform designed primarily to support teachers with planning, instructional resources, differentiation, communication, and efficiency. MagicSchool is teacher-facing.
13. What is MagicStudent?
MagicStudent is MagicSchool’s student-facing Artificial Intelligence platform. It is designed to help students practice skills and receive real-time feedback under direct teacher supervision. MagicStudent is not currently available for Broward County Public Schools students.
14. Will there be additional training if MagicStudent is used in schools?
Yes. If MagicStudent is approved for future student use, teachers will be required to complete additional training before turning on student-facing features. The training requirements, content, and procedures will be finalized before any student access is granted or shared in the classroom.
15. Will families be notified if a teacher uses MagicStudent with their child?
Yes. If the district approves MagicStudent for future student use, families will be informed before implementation. Communication procedures, parent information, and any available choices will be shared after related policy and procedures are finalized. For the latest updates please visit browardschools.ai.
16. Do Broward County Public Schools approved tools create content for students?
Approved student tools are to be used to coach, guide, and support students. Tools are not to replace student thinking, creativity, or create completed work for students to submit as their own.
17. Will Artificial Intelligence be used for grading?
Broward County Public Schools does not use Artificial Intelligence in place of a teacher to assign a final grade. An approved tool may assist with the teacher’s review, suggest follow-up instruction to strengthen student skills, and provide feedback during learning; however, the teacher remains responsible for reviewing student work, giving final feedback, and determining grades.
18. How are Artificial Intelligence tools selected and approved?
District-approved tools are reviewed for instructional value, privacy, safety, accessibility, age appropriateness, teacher training needs, responsible use, and alignment with district goals.
19. What are real-time threat detection and real-time moderation?
Real-time threat detection means a tool can alert adults quickly if a student interaction includes serious concerns such as self-harm or threats to others. Real-time moderation means the tool redirects or stops conversations that move into unsafe, inappropriate, or overly personal areas.
20. Who receives student safety alerts?
For approved student-facing tools, teachers receive alerts when a serious threat is detected. Depending on the level of concern, the alert may also be escalated to administrators and other trained personnel.
21. How is student information protected?
The district reviews approved tools for privacy and security requirements. Relevant protections include the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA), the Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), and the Protection of Pupil Rights Amendment (PPRA). Florida protects K-12 student data through the Student Online Personal Information Protection Act (Florida SOPIPA), State Board of Education Rule 6A-1.09550, and the Florida Information Protection Act (FIPA). Student information in approved tools stays within the Broward County Public Schools environment and is not used to train Artificial Intelligence.
22. Do student interactions train the Artificial Intelligence models?
No. Under current district requirements, approved Broward County Public Schools tools must not use our students’ interactions to train Artificial Intelligence models.
23. Are ChatGPT, Google AI, or other open tools used?
Open public tools such as ChatGPT and Google Artificial Intelligence features are not approved by Broward County Public Schools as student-facing instructional tools. They may still be accessible outside district-controlled settings. The district is reviewing controls for open public tools on school-issued devices, including devices used on home networks. Access may be more difficult to control when students use devices on home networks. Families are encouraged to remind students to use only teacher-directed, district-approved tools for schoolwork and to discuss safe, responsible technology use at home.
24. Can families preview or test approved tools?
Yes, preview and testing opportunities are being planned. The district is exploring possible regional sessions where parents and guardians could see or interact with tools that may be used in the future. Details have not yet been finalized and will be made available when they are. Latest updates are available at browardschools.ai.
25. Will Artificial Intelligence increase screen time?
The district recognizes family concerns about screen time. Artificial Intelligence is a tool, and use must have a clear learning purpose and should be balanced with reading, writing, discussion, collaboration, hands-on learning, movement, and direct interaction with teachers and classmates. Specific requirements continue to be refined and guided by data, policy, and instructional guidance.
26. How is Artificial Intelligence different from i-Ready and other education technology?
Artificial Intelligence is one type of educational technology. i-Ready is not an Artificial Intelligence tool. Families often connect these tools because they involve devices, screen time, homework, and classroom use. The district acknowledges concerns with screen time and student overdependence on technology and continues to listen to and address the concerns by constantly and regularly reviewing feedback, student data, appropriate use, and impact on student learning.
27. How will Artificial Intelligence support reading, writing, and social skills?
Artificial Intelligence can support reading and writing by providing feedback, helping teachers differentiate instruction, or presenting ideas in different ways. It is a tool that helps inform instruction; it does not replace discussion, human interaction, social development, or teacher guidance. The district’s position is that Artificial Intelligence should never replace human interaction.
28. Could Artificial Intelligence reduce critical thinking or creativity?
This is a valid concern. Students still need to read, write, solve problems, think independently, communicate clearly, and create original ideas. Our approach is for Artificial Intelligence to support and develop these skills, not to replace them, by teaching students to use Artificial Intelligence responsibly and ethically. Teachers remain central to instruction and responsible for setting expectations, guiding, and reviewing student work.
29. Will students be taught about the risks and limits of Artificial Intelligence?
Yes. The district is committed to responsible use. As part of our Artificial Intelligence vision, instruction will help students understand both how to use Artificial Intelligence and when not to use it. This includes checking accuracy, avoiding overreliance, protecting privacy, using tools ethically, and verifying generated information.
30. How does Artificial Intelligence help teachers without replacing them?
Artificial Intelligence is a tool that will help teachers save time with planning, resource creation, differentiation, communication, and administrative tasks. It will be used to support educators, not replace them. Teachers remain responsible for, and central to, individualized instruction, feedback, relationships, and decisions about student learning.
31. How will success be measured?
The district plans to review success through multiple measures, including usage, student outcomes, student engagement, teacher workload, teacher time saved, and whether tools support and enhance individualized student instruction. Data will be shared in district monthly reports to the School Board and public updates through Broward Powered by AI. Latest updates are available at browardschools.ai.
32. What would cause the district to stop using a tool?
Every instructional tool is evaluated on an ongoing basis. This applies to traditional and digital tools, including Artificial Intelligence tools. If evidence and data show that a tool does not support district goals, improve learning, protect students, or meet safety and privacy requirements, the district reconsiders, adjusts, replaces, suspends, or discontinues its use.
33. Are there costs for the district to use Khanmigo, Writable, or MagicSchool?
These tools do not currently cost the district additional funds to use. Khanmigo is through Khan Academy, Writable is connected to the adopted instructional materials, and MagicSchool was donated. Teacher training is provided through district staff, vendor-provided training, or agreements and does not cost the district, schools, or teachers extra. Long-term costs and value continue to be reviewed and assessed to ensure they meet district student learning goals.
34. How does Artificial Intelligence affect the district’s climate goals?
We are all concerned about the energy and environmental impact AI can have. The use of Artificial Intelligence can help us better track and improve the use of operational systems, such as lighting, heating, ventilation, air conditioning, and building systems, to help reduce energy use. Environmental impact remains an important question that we will continue reviewing.
35. How can families stay informed?
Families can visit the Broward Powered by AI website at browardschools.ai for updates, approved tools, parent resources, recordings, slide decks, family forum dates, community feedback opportunities, and accountability information.
36. How can parents and guardians participate in future decisions?
Families can complete district and school-based surveys, submit feedback through Broward Powered by AI, attend Community and Family Forums, participate in school advisory meetings, attend area or district advisory meetings, contact School Board members, attend School Board meetings, and share concerns through school and district channels.
37. What are the next Family Forum dates?
The next Family Forum is scheduled for August 27, 2026, with another forum expected on either September 23 or September 24, 2026. Families should check district communications and Broward Powered by AI for updates and posting of future sessions.
38. What if my family’s question was not answered during the forum?
Families are encouraged to continue submitting questions and feedback to the team through Broward Powered by AI. Please visit browardschools.ai.
39. How will data be used to make decisions about Artificial Intelligence tools, and who oversees those decisions?
Broward County Public Schools plans to look at more than numbers alone. The district’s monitoring process includes family feedback, student outcomes, student engagement, teacher workload, and possible overuse or unintended effects. Oversight includes the Instructional Innovation & Digital Learning Department, along with district leadership groups, tool review teams, educators, and other human decision makers. Broward County Public Schools is also a proud member of the SPARK initiative, led by Impact Florida in partnership with the University of Florida’s CS Everyone Center and in collaboration with Microsoft, Google, and other school districts. For more information about SPARK, please visit browardschools.ai/#event-spark.
40. What evidence will show whether Artificial Intelligence tools are helping students and teachers?
The district plans to review several measures, including student outcomes, engagement, teacher workload, time saved, and whether the tools support individualized learning. Families will also have opportunities to share feedback. Because implementation is still developing, the district will continue reviewing results and sharing updates rather than relying on one measure alone.
41. Will the district compare outcomes for students who use Artificial Intelligence tools with those who do not?
As tools become available to students, data will be collected and reviewed using a methodical approach to help inform future decisions. This review will include both quantitative and qualitative data and will consider students in the same grade levels and their performance on specific standards.
42. When will MagicStudent be available for students?
MagicStudent, the student-facing part of MagicSchool, is not currently available to Broward County Public Schools students. The district continues to review family feedback and future implementation. If access is approved later, teachers will receive training before providing access to students, and student use will be teacher directed and teacher monitored.
43. Are teachers being trained to use Artificial Intelligence responsibly and effectively?
Yes. The district is providing professional learning focused on responsible use, privacy, safety, instructional quality, and human oversight. Teachers will receive additional training before they can use MagicStudent with students. Artificial Intelligence tools are optional supports for teachers and do not replace their professional judgment.
44. What is the current cost of MagicSchool, Khanmigo, and HMH Writable?
These tools do not currently add extra costs to the district. MagicSchool access was provided through a donation, Khanmigo access is supported through Khan Academy and a tutoring grant for high school students, and HMH Writable is included with the adopted English language arts materials.
45. How will the district pay for these tools if free access, grants, or donations end?
The district continually monitors and reviews long-term funding plans. Before continuing or expanding a tool, the district reviews its educational value, student outcomes, total cost, available funding sources, and whether the tool is producing the expected results.
46. Are there additional requirements connected to the MagicSchool donation through the Community Foundation of Broward?
MagicSchool is supported through a donation. At this time, the district can provide only quantitative data because MagicSchool and MagicStudent have not yet been fully rolled out. As additional data becomes available, the district will share findings with funders and the community by posting reports on the Broward Powered by AI website: browardschools.ai.
47. How many schools currently use Prisms Virtual Reality?
Prisms Virtual Reality is an approved, teacher-directed immersive math and science tool. The number of participating schools at this time is 58 total schools, including all schools with middle grades (K-8 and 6-12 combinations) plus a small number of high schools and centers.
48. Do students use their own district login when accessing approved Artificial Intelligence tools?
Approved tools are made available through the student access portal, known as Clever. Students can access only the tools available for their grade level and instructional setting. Some features must also be assigned or enabled by a teacher.
49. Is a student’s work in MagicStudent private?
MagicStudent is not currently available to Broward County Public Schools students. Information entered into approved tools stays within the district environment and is not used to train Artificial Intelligence models. Approved tools allow authorized teachers or administrators to review student activity for instruction, safety, and appropriate use.
50. Are students’ Individualized Education Programs (IEP) entered into MagicSchool?
At this time, details from a student’s Individualized Education Program are not automatically loaded into MagicSchool. The district currently has this feature turned off. The district team is reviewing features and processes to ensure that, if activated, the feature will align with district and state standards and follow Broward County Public Schools privacy requirements.
51. Does district approval of Artificial Intelligence change the rules for using Teachers Pay Teachers resources?
Teachers Pay Teachers is an online marketplace where prekindergarten through grade 12 educators buy, sell, and share educational resources. These resources may not align with Florida standards or district requirements. By comparison, Artificial Intelligence tools approved and implemented by Broward County Public Schools are vetted and reviewed for effectiveness, safety, and alignment with Florida state standards.
52. Are schools or employees rewarded for using Artificial Intelligence tools?
Artificial Intelligence tools such as MagicSchool are optional supports for teachers. Their use is based on a clear instructional or operational purpose, not on earning a reward. Microsoft is supporting the district’s effort to improve operations and productivity by offering an incentive to encourage the use of Copilot. The incentive is awarded to the school with the highest use, and the funds go directly to that school to support its needs.
53. Can a teacher set limits or parameters when students use Khanmigo?
Khanmigo guides students step by step rather than simply providing answers. Teachers determine how it is used, assign activities or features, and establish classroom expectations for when and how students may use it. Teachers can also enable Focus Mode, a feature that allows teachers to disable Khanmigo for the entire class or individual students for up to four hours at a time.
54. How will the district respond if state law requires parent permission before students use Artificial Intelligence?
The district will follow any final legal and policy requirements. The Broward Powered by AI site is intended to provide parent notices, available choices, contacts, and updates. Because requirements may change, families will receive clear information before any student-facing Artificial Intelligence tool is introduced. To stay up to date, please visit browardschools.ai.
55. Is Microsoft Copilot used by students in the classroom?
No. Microsoft Copilot Premium is primarily a staff-facing tool and is not available to the general Broward County Public Schools student population. A small group of approved high school students in a specific technology pathway uses a monitored version under staff supervision.
56. Can students open approved Artificial Intelligence programs on their own, such as HMH Writable?
Access depends on the tool. Khanmigo is available to eligible secondary students through the district environment. Teachers determine when HMH Writable is assigned or enabled for student work. MagicStudent is not currently available. Student-facing tools and their use remain connected to grade level, instruction, teacher oversight, and district approval.
57. How will the district prevent students from being unfairly accused of using Artificial Intelligence?
A student should not be judged only by an automated detector or by how polished the work appears. Teachers should review the student’s process, drafts, sources, assignment expectations, and explanation of the work before reaching a conclusion. The district’s guiding approach keeps human judgment and fair educational decisions at the center. The district also has a task force reviewing procedures to help ensure that tools used to identify copied or Artificial Intelligence-authored work are used appropriately, include safeguards, and have proper oversight.
58. How will Artificial Intelligence tools support students with disabilities and other individual learning needs?
Accessibility is one of the district’s tool-selection principles. Artificial Intelligence may help teachers adjust reading levels, provide different ways to access content, and create more individualized support. Approved tools must pass an accessibility review, and they do not replace a student’s Individualized Education Program, Section 504 plan, accommodations, specialized instruction, or support from educators and service providers. The Exceptional Student Education & Support Services Department is involved in reviewing tools to help ensure that district standards are properly incorporated into any tool released for teacher use.
59. Does using more Artificial Intelligence conflict with the district’s goal of reducing screen time?
These goals do not have to conflict when technology is used for a clear, limited learning purpose. The district recognizes concerns about total screen time. Artificial Intelligence supports instruction; it does not replace reading, writing, discussion, movement, hands-on learning, or interaction between teachers and students. The district strives for a balanced approach to student learning and the use of technology, and continues to monitor and review its use, adjusting based on data and input from parents and teachers.
60. Do approved Artificial Intelligence tools allow adults to review what students enter, and who can see it?
Approved student-facing tools include monitoring, moderation, and flagging features. Teachers oversee student use, and safety concerns are shared with appropriate school administrators. The exact level of visibility depends on the tool and the educational or safety purpose. Student information is protected, remains within the district environment, and is shared only with authorized staff who need it.
61. Can parents review their child’s prompts and work in approved Artificial Intelligence tools?
As with other student work, parents may ask the teacher or school to explain how a tool is being used and may ask questions about their child’s work.
62. Will parents need to give permission before schools use Artificial Intelligence with their child?
The district will follow final state law, School Board policy, and district procedures. Current family resources are designed to provide notice, available choices, contacts, and updates. Because requirements may change, families will receive clear information before a student-facing tool is introduced. To stay up to date, please visit browardschools.ai.
63. Who trains the Artificial Intelligence systems used by Broward County Public Schools?
Artificial Intelligence models are developed and trained by vendors using large data sets before the district approves them. Broward County Public Schools reviews tools for privacy, safety, accessibility, reliability, and instructional value. District-approved tools do not use Broward County Public Schools students or their data to train Artificial Intelligence models.
64. How does a teacher provide feedback when students use HMH Writable?
Teachers can add an assignment rubric for students to use while writing. HMH Writable can provide color-coded suggestions on grammar, structure, word choice, and alignment with the rubric before the student submits the work. The tool provides an additional layer of support, but the teacher still reviews the work and provides final feedback, whether during class, after the student submits the work, or both.
65. Can students use Khanmigo when they need tutoring help?
Yes. All students in grades 6 through 8 currently have access to Khanmigo, and students in grades 9 through 12 have access through the Florida Tutoring Advantage Grant. Khanmigo provides step-by-step guidance, study strategies, and links to Khan Academy practice. Teachers may also assign additional activities or enable additional features.
66. When students use MagicStudent or another approved Artificial Intelligence tool, will it check whether their answers are accurate?
The way a tool responds depends on the tool and the activity selected by the teacher. MagicStudent is designed to guide students through their thinking by asking questions and providing prompts instead of giving them an answer. It can help a student reconsider an answer or think through the next step, but it does not replace the teacher’s review or judgment. Teachers determine the learning goal and how the tool should support the activity.
67. Will MagicStudent grade student work?
No. MagicStudent is intended to coach and guide students, not independently grade their work. A teacher remains responsible for reviewing student work, providing feedback, and making grading decisions.
68. How does MagicStudent support students without doing the thinking for them?
MagicStudent uses a question-based approach similar to tutoring. It may ask follow-up questions, prompt students to explain their thinking, or guide them toward information they should consider. The goal is to support the student’s reasoning rather than create the work or provide the final answer.
69. Can teachers decide what MagicStudent should focus on during an activity?
Yes. Teachers have the ability to define the purpose of the activity and the type of thinking students should practice. This is important because accuracy may not be the goal in every assignment; for example, some activities may focus on building an argument, practicing creativity, developing fluency, or explaining reasoning. The teacher remains responsible for setting the expectations and deciding how the tool supports student learning.
70. Can Artificial Intelligence support teacher planning without creating the lesson for the teacher?
Yes. An Artificial Intelligence tool can be set up to coach a teacher through the planning process by asking questions about the lesson, student needs, learning goals, and instructional decisions. This approach supports the teacher’s thinking instead of replacing it or automatically creating a lesson plan without teacher input.
71. Will students learn how Artificial Intelligence works, including generative Artificial Intelligence and Artificial Intelligence agents, rather than only learning how to use it as a productivity tool?
Yes. Artificial Intelligence literacy is an important part of the district’s approach. In elementary school, students learn the ideas behind how Artificial Intelligence works without using student-facing Artificial Intelligence tools. Through age-appropriate activities, students learn about patterns, data, predictions, checking information, privacy, fairness, and how technology can help solve problems. As students grow, this foundation prepares them to understand more advanced ideas and to use approved tools thoughtfully and responsibly. The goal is not simply to teach students how to use a tool; it is to help them understand how the technology works, what it can and cannot do, and why human judgment remains essential.
72. How will the district help students and teachers understand that Artificial Intelligence is a tool, not a person that thinks or knows things the way people do?
The district’s Artificial Intelligence literacy message is clear: Artificial Intelligence is a tool, not a person. It looks for patterns in information and may use those patterns to make a prediction or suggestion. It can be useful and still be wrong. Elementary lessons build this understanding through age-appropriate activities that focus on patterns, data, predictions, and checking answers rather than using student-facing Artificial Intelligence tools. For secondary students, approved tools are used in teacher-guided settings, and teachers and students are expected to review results, question the information, and make the final decisions. Human thinking, evidence, and responsibility remain most important.
73. How will students learn to recognize when an Artificial Intelligence tool agrees with or flatters the user instead of providing accurate information?
Students need to understand that a confident, friendly, or agreeable response is not always a correct response. The district’s Artificial Intelligence literacy approach teaches students to question technology, check information, and use reliable sources before accepting or sharing an answer. Families can reinforce this thinking by asking, “Could the computer be wrong?”, “What information did it use?”, and “How can we check the answer?” Students should compare the response with trusted evidence, consider whether important viewpoints are missing, and ask an adult or teacher when they are unsure. The goal is to help students use Artificial Intelligence thoughtfully without treating agreement as proof that an answer is accurate.

Myth

Artificial Intelligence will replace teachers.

Fact

Teachers remain central to learning and decision making in Broward County Public Schools.

Myth

Elementary students are currently using district-approved Artificial Intelligence tools.

Fact

No district-approved student-facing Artificial Intelligence tools are currently available for elementary students in kindergarten through grade 5.

Myth

Artificial Intelligence will make human relationships less important.

Fact

Broward County Public Schools believes human connection and relationships are essential to student learning and development.

Myth

Students have unrestricted access to district Artificial Intelligence tools.

Fact

Under current district practice, approved student use is grade-based, teacher directed, teacher monitored, and limited to supported instructional settings. Because students may still encounter public tools outside district-controlled settings, families should talk with their children about safe and responsible use.

MagicStudent is currently open for Broward County Public Schools students.

Fact

MagicStudent is not currently available to Broward County Public Schools students. If it is approved in the future, notifications will be shared with families and trained teachers will direct and monitor its use under district oversight.

Myth

Artificial Intelligence will grade students without teacher input.

Fact

Broward County Public Schools does not use Artificial Intelligence in place of a teacher to assign a final grade. Artificial Intelligence may help support feedback. Teachers remain responsible for review, feedback, and grading decisions.

Student data is used to train district-approved Artificial Intelligence tools.

Fact

Under current district requirements, student information in approved tools must remain within the Broward County Public Schools environment and cannot be used to train Artificial Intelligence models.

The district is relying only on vendors to decide whether tools are safe.

Artificial Intelligence automatically means more screen time.

How student data is protected

The protections behind every AI decision. Read the plain-language answer first, then open the full document. The District is presenting a comprehensive AI policy to the School Board for approval. Once ratified, the approved policy will be linked here.

Sample answers for layout. Final wording to be confirmed by BCPS legal and data governance, including any Florida-specific privacy law.
What information is collected, and why?
Approved tools collect only what is needed to deliver the educational service. Information is used for that purpose alone, not for advertising or any unrelated commercial use.
Where is data stored, and who has access?
Data stays within the district's protected systems and approved vendor environments governed by data privacy agreements. Access is limited by role to the staff who need it for instruction and support.
Can AI train on my child's information?
No. Approved tools are configured so student and staff information is not used to train public AI models. This is a condition of approval for every tool listed on this site.
What are my rights as a parent?
Parents have the right to know what AI tools are being used in their child's education and how they are being used, and to request that their child be excluded from the use of AI where practicable. How are tools checked before students use them?
All student-facing tools are subjected to a rigorous review process to ensure they include protections beyond the already stringent BCPS data privacy and security provisions. Each tool must demonstrate real-time threat detection and moderation, with immediate notification to teachers and administrators of any inappropriate usage. Vendors also sign data privacy agreements and must meet COPPA requirements for younger students; approved tools may not sell student data or use it for targeted advertising.
Are AI detectors used to accuse students?
No. AI detection tools can be unreliable and biased, so they are not relied upon to make decisions about a student. Academic integrity is handled through clear expectations and honest conversation.

AI literacy

Understanding AI, for students and families

Broward teaches students not just to use AI, but to understand it, question it, and use it responsibly. The approach is built on the AI4K12 Five Big Ideas, the leading K-12 AI literacy framework.

The Five Big Ideas in AI

Perception

AI senses the world through cameras and microphones and turns signals into meaning.

Representation & Reasoning

AI builds models of the world to work through problems, but does not think like a person.

Learning

Computers learn by finding patterns in large amounts of data supplied by people.

Natural Interaction

Interacting naturally with people takes many kinds of knowledge that AI still lacks.

Societal Impact

AI can help and harm. Biased data can leave some people worse served, so ethics matter.

What students learn, by grade band

Elementary

  • Recognize where AI shows up in everyday life and that it is a human-made tool.
  • Understand computers learn differently from people, by finding patterns in data.
  • Begin reasoning about fairness, and that altered photos and videos exist.

Middle

  • Train and test a simple model and examine its data for bias.
  • Understand how recommendation algorithms shape what they see online.
  • Define AI and generative AI and weigh benefits and drawbacks.

High

  • Carry out a full machine-learning process and evaluate the data critically.
  • Judge AI against fairness, transparency, accountability, and privacy.
  • Evaluate AI-generated content for accuracy, bias, origin, and citation ethics.

What the forum covered

Student safety drives every decision

MagicSchool & MagicStudent: readiness, safeguards, and implementation

The Division of Academics' response to School Board Agenda Item 1 concerns about MagicSchool AI, MagicStudent, student safety, data privacy, teacher control, parent transparency, training, and accountability.

MagicSchool and MagicStudent Readiness, Safeguards, and Implementation ResponseFinal Full Report · Division of Academics, July 2026
Read the Executive Brief and key findings

Division of Academics position. The evidence shows Broward County Public Schools has already established the core privacy, contractual, training, and oversight structures needed to proceed responsibly. MagicStudent has been deactivated for Broward since the agreement was executed. BCPS staff testing found MagicSchool redirected or stopped every harmful prompt attempt across all 19 adversarial test scenarios; no student would have received an unsafe response in any test case. Rather than an indefinite pause, the report recommends an accountability-based implementation model with readiness gates, public reporting, parent communication, teacher training, and student access controls.

MagicStudent remains off. A decision has been made that MagicStudent will stay turned off for Broward until readiness requirements are met. For SY 2026–2027, only secondary teachers who opt in will be required to complete MagicStudent training, which grants them access to create and control student rooms — including which tools are available, activity monitoring, and the ability to open, pause, lock, close, or remove students.

Two distinct safety mechanisms. Redirection is real-time student protection: when a student attempts a harmful prompt, the platform refuses to engage, stops the conversation, and where appropriate directs the student to a trusted adult or crisis resource. In testing, this worked in every case. Flagging is a separate, back-end administrative notification layer, with sensitivity configurable by the District. The widely-circulated “31%” figure refers only to which of the 19 redirected test conversations triggered an administrative flag under the platform's default, out-of-the-box settings — it does not describe an unsafe student response, since there were none.

Current teacher-facing momentum (through May 20, 2026). 4,318 teacher users active; 60,423 AI interactions; 217 schools active; 33 professional learning sessions delivered.

Public message. “BCPS is not replacing teachers with AI. We are implementing a teacher-controlled, policy-governed, contractually protected, and transparently communicated instructional AI support system. MagicStudent is currently off. Student-facing use in SY 2026–2027 will be limited to trained secondary teachers, and only after readiness conditions are met.”

For staff and leaders

AI guidance for district and school leadership

Artificial intelligence for a district or school leader is best framed not just as a classroom tool, but as a system-level capability that affects instruction, operations, equity, and community trust. This guidance is written for clerical staff, specialists and facilitators, and leaders, not for families. See Parent Resources and Our Principles for the family-facing versions of this guidance.

Ethical AI use: remember the 3 principles

Fairness: treat all voices and perspectives with respect. Accuracy: always fact-check AI-generated information. Transparency: be clear when and how AI is used.

Role-based guidance

For clerical staff

DO

  • Draft emails, summarize meetings, suggest next steps
  • Organize and classify documents
  • Provide quick research or background information

DO NOT

  • Send communications without human review
  • Make decisions about personnel, students, or policy
  • Handle or store confidential data outside authorized systems

For specialists and facilitators

DO

  • Improve training design and professional learning materials
  • Analyze data for continuous improvement
  • Support project management, scheduling, and coordination

DO NOT

  • Let AI make instructional or professional learning decisions alone
  • Input confidential student, staff, or district data into unapproved tools
  • Select instructional resources or partnerships without human evaluation

For leaders

DO

  • Synthesize data, forecast scenarios, generate strategic briefs
  • Draft memos, meeting summaries, and community updates
  • Keep the human in the loop and always review output

DO NOT

  • Allow AI to make personnel, staffing, or evaluative decisions
  • Rely on AI to interpret Board policies, contracts, or statutes
  • Use AI as the source of truth for improvement or technology decisions
