Parent AI Symposium 2026

On June 5, 2026, the District brought parents, students, educators, and school leaders together to learn how AI is shaping education and how Broward is using it responsibly.

Hosted at Nova High School in Davie, the Parent AI Symposium was an in-person event designed to help families understand how artificial intelligence is transforming the classroom and how to support their students in an increasingly technology-driven world. Attendees explored both the benefits and the concerns around AI, with a main session focused on what families need to know.

A highlight of the day was students themselves demonstrating how they use AI in their learning, alongside district leaders answering questions directly from parents.

"This is an opportunity to really teach people about how Broward County Public Schools plans to use AI in the classroom and outside the classroom as well."Dr. Howard Hepburn, Superintendent, Broward County Public Schools
"It's really important for leaders and policy makers to anticipate the big changes that AI is going to bring and prepare our students and our families for an AI workforce."Sarah Leonardi, School Board Chair

Community · Event

Family Forum

A series of three parent-facing live sessions, each recorded for families who could not attend live. Broward County Public Schools hosted its first Family Forum on Artificial Intelligence to share clear information with families about how AI may support teaching and learning, while addressing community questions and concerns.

Recording of the July 29, 2026 Family Forum: Understanding AI and Its Use in Broward Schools, hosted by Thomas Correll.

Held July 29, 2026, the forum brought together parents, families, community representatives, educators, and school leaders to discuss how Broward County Public Schools is using AI responsibly. It was created in response to parent feedback and designed as the first in a series of community conversations focused on AI, screen time, student safety, instructional technology, and responsible innovation.

District staff introduced families to the fundamentals of artificial intelligence — what AI is, how it works, and how approved instructional tools differ from publicly available AI platforms — and covered district-approved tools such as Khanmigo and HMH Writable, student-privacy safeguards, and the vetting process required before any student-facing AI tool reaches a classroom. Parents took part in an extended question-and-answer session on AI and grading, opt-out opportunities, monitoring, teacher training, approved versus non-approved tools, and how the district measures success.

"This work is not replacing teachers. It is not replacing human connection. And it is certainly not allowing technology to make decisions for our children... Our responsibility is helping students understand these tools, use them appropriately, and develop the critical-thinking skills needed for the future."Mr. Thomas Correll, Director, Instructional Innovation and Digital Learning
"Broward's vision for artificial intelligence is not about replacing teachers. It never has been. It never will be... Our vision is simple. It's human intelligence first, it's artificial intelligence second."Dr. Fabian Cone, Chief Academic Officer
"When it became clear that there were community concerns that really needed attention, answers, and accountability, it was important to start addressing and acknowledging them. That's how this Family Forum came about."Jacqui Luscombe, ESE Advisory Council Chair

What the forum covered

Student safety drives every decision

Before an AI tool can be used with students, it must pass extensive privacy, security, moderation, and safety reviews. District leaders shared that approved tools such as MagicSchool and MagicStudent have monitoring features, real-time safeguards, and meet student-privacy compliance requirements.

AI supports teaching, not replaces it

Teachers remain responsible for instruction, grading, and relationships with students. AI tools are designed to help educators save time on administrative tasks, personalize learning, and spend more time supporting students.

A thoughtful, age-appropriate approach

Elementary students currently do not have access to district-approved student-facing AI tools. Student access, when available, is tied to grade level, teacher training, instructional purpose, and ongoing oversight.

Continuous improvement through monitoring

The district plans to monitor implementation, review usage data, gather community feedback, and evaluate outcomes to ensure AI tools continue to support student achievement and district goals.

Community · Event · District 4

Back to School AI: What Parents Need to Know

On August 17, 2026, District 4 hosted a Back to School AI parent event at El Mariachi Restaurant in Coral Springs, walking families through how AI is shaping education at BCPS, how to keep kids safe online, and practical ways to support learning and creativity at home.

Presented by Dr. Fabian Cone (Chief Academic Officer), Thomas Correll (Director, Instructional Innovation & Digital Learning), Jaclyn Sherman (Professional Development Specialist), and Nathan Clock (Instructional & Digital Materials Analyst) from the Division of Academics, the session (titled "Broward Powered by AI: From Vision to System Readiness") walked families through why AI matters now, the five commitments to every family, which AI tools are approved and for which grade bands, and what has been completed so far — from the AI Task Force and School Board Resolution No. 26-132 to the June 5 Symposium and Family Forum sessions.

Recognition

Broward selected for Impact Florida's SPARK cadre

Broward County Public Schools is one of six Florida districts selected to join SPARK, Supporting Practical AI for Real K-12 Classrooms, an 18-month statewide initiative examining and supporting responsible AI use in education.

SPARK is led by Impact Florida in partnership with the University of Florida's CS Everyone Center, in collaboration with Microsoft and Google. Through the program, district teams work on both policy governance and practice: developing or refining guidance on ethics, data privacy, procurement, academic integrity, and responsible use, while testing practical instructional approaches rooted in real classroom needs.

The other districts selected for the first SPARK cadre are Citrus County School District, School District of Manatee County, School District of Palm Beach County, Polk County Public Schools, and Putnam County School District (in partnership with NEFEC).

"AI is changing education faster than ever before, and districts shouldn't have to navigate that change alone. Through SPARK, we're creating a collaborative space where district leaders can learn from one another, build coherent systems, and develop thoughtful approaches that put students, educators, and communities at the center of AI implementation."Dr. Nadine Ebri, SPARK Program and Instructional Lead Consultant

District teams will assess their current AI readiness, collaborate with peers and national experts, strengthen policy and governance, pilot instructional innovations, and develop coherent approaches that align leadership, technology, and teaching.
